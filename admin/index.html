🚨 Watchtower

Problems found automatically — resolves itself when fixed. Two checks watch a pair of dates: Offer expires before completion catches an offer that dies before the completion it is meant to fund, so it has to be re-issued; Completing inside old ERC catches a retention case completing while the client's old deal is still inside its Early Repayment Charge, so they pay to leave early. Client emails unanswered is counted per client — one row per person waiting on a reply, not one per message.

Clear a whole run of them at once. Tick the alerts you have judged — or use Select all on a check's own heading to take every row of that one check — then Snooze 7 days, Snooze 30 days or Dismiss on the bar. One confirmation for the batch, one optional reason applied to all of them, and the list repaints once at the end. Neither verb fixes anything: a snooze hides an alert until the date passes, and a dismissal closes it — either way, a problem that is still there is raised again the next time the checks run. A reason is required when the batch includes a critical alert, and it is written onto that alert's case, exactly as it is when you snooze or dismiss one on its own.

ⓘ How the queue, review requests and reminders behave

Nothing composes new emails for you: rate-end reminders, review requests and fee requests are queued by you from a case, and each one sends on its own — pressing an action on a case never releases anybody else's queue. Running the whole firm's queue early confirms the count and recipients first.

Review requests are dripped: the automation queues at most 5 per run, oldest completion first, so a back-catalogue of completed cases doesn't land on your review page in one afternoon. The rest wait for the next run. · Annual reviews send no email. When the annual-review setting is on, each completion anniversary creates a call task (“Annual review call — …”) for the case's adviser; it appears in My Day and on the case, and nothing here.

An unanswered review request is followed up once. About a week after a review request that was actually sent and never answered (the exact gap is the “Review reminder if unanswered” setting), one reminder is queued — shown below as Review reminder (2nd ask). One per case ever, never a third, and it comes out of the same 5-a-run budget as the requests, taken only after them: a client who has never been asked always goes before one who is being asked twice. If somebody should not be asked again, cancel the row while it is still queued: press Cancel on it, confirm, and the email never sends — its status becomes cancelled, and where the row belongs to a case the cancellation is written onto that case's timeline with your name. · An unhappy answer creates work, not an email. A score of 6 or below raises a call-back task for the case's adviser and writes the client's own words onto the case timeline; nothing is sent back to the client automatically.

Read one before it goes. Every queued or failed row has a Preview this email fold showing the subject and, where the wording is already written, the text itself — shown as plain formatting only, with no links, images or styling loaded from it. Stop several at once. Tick the queued or failed rows you do not want sent and use Cancel selected on the bar: one confirmation for the whole batch, and a cancelled email never sends and is never retried. Retry stays what it always was — it only ever acts on rows that have already failed, and says how many of your selection that is.

Data & backup

One file containing every row this back office holds — clients, cases, tasks, notes, documents, appointments, leads, the email and SMS queues, fact-finds, introducers, settings, logins, alerts, absences and the audit log. It is a backup you control: keep it somewhere safe outside this system, so the firm's record of itself does not depend on this application or its hosting continuing to exist.

It is not encrypted. It is a plain JSON file with your clients' names, contact details, dates of birth and financial figures in it. Treat the file exactly as you would treat the database: somewhere access-controlled, not a shared drive and not an email attachment.

This is a copy, not a migration: nothing here restores it, and no other system reads this format. It is what you would hand a developer, an auditor or a successor firm.

The password vault is not in this file. Company logins and passwords are deliberately excluded — an unencrypted backup is the last place they belong. Manage them on the Vault page instead.
