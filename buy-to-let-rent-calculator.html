Calculating rent for a buy-to-let mortgage

Most buy-to-let lenders size the loan on rent rather than on your salary. The basic calculation is:

Rent needed each month = mortgage amount × stress rate ÷ 12 × rental cover ratio

For illustration only, using a hypothetical stress rate of 5% (not a current rate): a £200,000 mortgage gives monthly interest of about £833. At 125% cover the rent needed is about £1,042 a month; at 145% it is about £1,208. Lenders use the rent their valuer confirms the property can achieve, which may differ from the rent you expect, and the calculation is usually made on an interest-only basis even if you choose a repayment mortgage.

What is ICR (interest cover ratio)?

The interest cover ratio (ICR), also called the rental cover ratio, is the monthly rent divided by the monthly mortgage interest at the lender’s stress rate, shown as a percentage. A result of 125% means the rent is one and a quarter times the stressed interest.

The ratio a lender asks for often depends on how the income is taxed. Individual landlords cannot deduct mortgage interest from rental income in the way they once could; instead they receive a tax credit at the basic rate. Higher- and additional-rate taxpayers therefore keep less of the rent after tax, so lenders often test them at 145%. Basic-rate taxpayers are often tested at 125%. Limited companies pay corporation tax and can generally treat mortgage interest as a business cost, so lenders often apply 125% to them too. Some lenders use other ratios, for example for HMOs or holiday lets. NexMoney does not provide taxation advice; please speak to a qualified tax adviser or accountant about your position.

Stress rates and pay rates

The pay rate is the interest rate you actually pay on the mortgage. The stress rate is the rate the lender uses for the rental cover test, and it is often higher than the pay rate — for example the pay rate plus a margin, or a minimum floor rate. On fixed rates of five years or more, lenders are often allowed to test at, or close to, the pay rate, which can mean a given rent supports a larger loan. Stress rates vary by lender and product and change over time, which is why this calculator asks you to enter one rather than suggesting a figure.

Top-slicing and other ways to bridge a shortfall

If the rent falls short of the test, some lenders allow top-slicing: using surplus personal income to make up the difference, subject to their affordability checks and usually a minimum income. Other options can include a larger deposit, a longer fixed rate that is tested nearer the pay rate, or a different ownership structure. Not every lender offers these, and criteria vary.

Gross yield and what it leaves out

Gross yield is the annual rent divided by the property value. It is a quick way to compare properties, but it ignores letting and management fees, maintenance, insurance, service charges, void periods and tax, so your net return will be lower. Lenders do not lend on yield; they use the rental cover test.

Who this calculator is for

This rental income calculator is designed for first-time landlords checking a purchase and for existing landlords testing a remortgage or a new addition to a portfolio. It does not include fees added to the loan, portfolio-wide stress tests for landlords with four or more mortgaged properties, or lender-specific rules. Buy-to-Let Rent FAQs

Your questions, answered

How do I calculate the rent needed for a buy-to-let mortgage?

Multiply the mortgage amount by the lender’s stress rate, divide by 12 to get the monthly stressed interest, then multiply by the rental cover ratio, such as 125% or 145%. The result is the minimum monthly rent the lender may want to see. Lenders’ ratios and stress rates vary.

What is a good interest cover ratio?

Lenders commonly ask for 125% or 145%, depending on your tax position and whether you buy personally or through a limited company. Meeting a lender’s ratio does not guarantee a mortgage: your credit history, experience, the property and other criteria are also assessed.

Why do higher-rate taxpayers need 145% rental cover?

Individual landlords now receive only a basic-rate tax credit for mortgage interest, so higher- and additional-rate taxpayers keep less of the rent after tax. Many lenders allow for this by asking for a higher rental cover ratio, often 145%. NexMoney does not provide taxation advice.

What stress rate do lenders use?

It varies by lender and product. The stress rate is often higher than the rate you pay, although on fixed rates of five years or more lenders are often allowed to test at or near the pay rate. That is why this calculator asks you to enter the rate you want to test.

Can I still borrow if the rent does not cover the test?

Possibly. Some lenders allow top-slicing with surplus personal income, and a larger deposit or a longer fixed rate can also change the result. These options are subject to lender criteria and affordability.
